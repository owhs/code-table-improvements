Computed 2026-10-14 (exact where marked). Files: README, codes.json, table_classes.csv, table_maxcode.csv, check_codes.py, related work.

1. The problem

A word x is a binary vector of length n and weight w. A receiver that can only measure intensities sees the power signature Px(k) = |∑j xj e-2πijk/n|2, k = 1..⌊n/2⌋ (equivalently the cyclic autocorrelation, i.e. the multiset of cyclic differences, of the support). Cyclic shifts, reflections and homometric (Z-related) partners have identical signatures; for w = n/2 a word and its complement do too. Define the normalised spectral distance d(x,y) = ∑k |Px(k) - Py(k)| / w2. A spectral code C(n,w,θ) is a set of weight-w words with pairwise d ≥ θ. Let S(n,w,θ) be the largest size. S(n,w,θ) ≤ c(n,w), the number of distinct signatures; equality holds exactly for θ ≤ δ(n,w), the smallest distance between two classes. The ordinary constant-weight code problem A(n,d,w) uses Hamming distance and counts shifts as different words; here a shift-closed code of any size is worthless.

7. Link to hyperdimensional computing (HDC) and optical orthogonal codes

In HDC an item is bound to a role by a cyclic shift (permutation) or circular convolution, items are bundled by superposition, and the readout correlates the bundle against a codebook (a resonator network iterates this). With sparse binary constant-weight vectors the bundle is the integer sum of shifted codewords and the readout scores are the circular cross-correlations with the codebook: the same interference as optical CDMA. Three consequences, one of them measured below.

  • Codebooks whose shifted words pairwise overlap in at most one position (optical orthogonal codes, correlation ≤ 1) have a counting guarantee: a true item scores at least w, a false hypothesis at most T, so T ≤ w-1 bundled items are read out exactly. The size of such a codebook is limited by the Johnson-type bound floor((n-1)/(w(w-1))).
  • Rosenblatt-Seymour (A = U+V against B = U-V) says binding with V and with the reversal of V give words with the same power spectrum: a magnitude-only readout cannot distinguish them. This is the homometric ambiguity of section 1 seen as an unbinding ambiguity.
  • Difference sets are the binary analogue of the unit-magnitude (FHRR) role vectors: perfect autocorrelation means the shifts of one word are quasi-orthogonal, which is ideal for roles but, by section 6, worthless as an alphabet for an intensity-only receiver (all shifts share one signature). Roles want flat spectra; alphabets for phase-blind readout want spread-out spectra.

8. Notes

  • All sizes in sections 3 are lower bounds by explicit codes (files) and, where marked exact, upper bounds by the integer program. One cell may be open (lb-ub).
  • c(n,w) agrees with the number of bracelets for w ≤ 3 in every cell computed (n ≤ 30) and falls below it first for w = 4 (n = 12: the pair {0,1,4,6}, {0,1,3,7}).
