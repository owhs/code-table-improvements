Notes

  • A(18,4): the Gray image of a Z4 code of length 9 and minimum Lee distance 4, made of 96 cosets of a 64-element submodule with all entries in {0,2}. A(17,4) is the half with last coordinate 1, that coordinate deleted; A(16,3) is that code punctured. Construction and coset representatives: binary folder.
  • A(31,10,12): 5 orbits of Z29 ⋊ Z7 on 29 coordinates, with 2 fixed points. A(32,10,10): orbits of Z31 ⋊ Z5 on 31 coordinates, with 1 fixed point.
  • 5-ary: 30 and 36 cosets of a doubled code (a,a) with sum(a) = 0. The published 17500 and 4375 have the same form with 28 and 35 cosets, so this is one more coset, not a new idea.
  • N(6,8,6), N(7,7,6) follow from N(5,9,6): keep one or two ternary coordinates to their two most frequent symbols.
  • The mixed codes were compared with your page only.

3. A new table: spectral-signature codes

Bounds for spectral-signature codes, laid out like your pages.

A receiver that sees only the power spectrum |DFT|2 of a binary word cannot tell cyclic shifts, reflections or homometric words apart. The 13 shifts of the difference set {0,1,3,9}, for instance, give A(13,6,4) = 13 but a single spectrum. S(n,w,θ) is the largest set of weight-w words whose spectra differ by at least θ; the page has it for n ≤ 20, with 239 of 252 entries proven optimal by integer programming.

As far as I can find, this table is new. A literature search found nothing that defines codes by a spectral distance or tabulates S(n,w,θ): the nearest work (coding for the turnpike and beltway problems, phaseless and intensity-only codebooks, cyclically permutable and optical orthogonal codes) uses Hamming or correlation constraints over shifts and keeps homometric words apart. The counts of distinct spectra on the same page are not new for n ≤ 24; they follow from Jedrzejewski and Johnson's enumeration of Z-related sets (2013), and agree with it. A companion note does the same with the triple correlation (bispectrum), which also separates homometric words; up to n = 18 its only collisions are mirror pairs, of the kind given by Keleti and Kolountzakis. Details: related work.

If you think it belongs alongside your tables, I would be glad to keep it up to date.
